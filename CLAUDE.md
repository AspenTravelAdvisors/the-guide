## Positioning: the idea underneath the voice

The Guide is the AI that luxury travel advisors work from, opened up to the traveler. Advisor AI is getting attention: Fora's tools (Sidekick, Via) helped it reach a $1B valuation in July 2026, but they sit behind an advisor login and Fora is deliberately not building for consumers. The Guide holds the same conviction from the other side of the desk. The traveler gets the advisor's knowledge directly: vetted Virtuoso inventory, the amenities, the places Aspen has actually been. Then a real advisor at Aspen Travel Advisors books it. **Open to anyone, built for ultra-luxury.**

Treat this as a point to remember, not a line to repeat:
- **It shapes tone, it isn't copy.** In Peterman captions, voiceovers, Guide takes, answers pages, the Guide prompt, app notes and branding, let it set the posture: an insider quietly letting the traveler in on what advisors know. Most pieces should never mention AI, advisor tools or this idea. They should just *sound* like insider knowledge.
- **Say it outright only where positioning is the point**, and only once: the homepage hero, the intro or about copy, onboarding, and pitch or press material.
- **Never name Fora, Sidekick or Via in traveler-facing copy**, and never imply an affiliation. The network is Virtuoso, through Aspen Travel Advisors. Fora is context for internal, investor or press framing only ("the market Fora validated").
- **Claim only what's real:** vetted inventory, Virtuoso amenities, "Been here" visits, a human who books. Don't claim live pricing or availability unless the feature exists.
- **The quiet contrast is with general chatbots.** They don't know the amenities, haven't been there, and can't book with perks. Imply that; don't name or knock them in traveler-facing copy.
- **Tagline seeds** (rotate, use sparingly, never stack): "The AI luxury advisors use. Now it's yours." · "Ask what the insiders ask. Book with the people who've been there." · "Advisor-grade intelligence, open to every traveler."
